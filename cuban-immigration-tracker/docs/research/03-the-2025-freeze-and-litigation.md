## 1. Executive summary (the short answer for the project owner)

1. **Two different government "freezes" stopped a Cuban CHNV parolee's green card (Form I-485 under the Cuban Adjustment Act) and work permit (Form I-765), and federal courts ended both.** They are keyed on different things — the first on *how you entered* (parole program), the second on *where you were born / your nationality* — and mixing them up is the most common error in community discussion. [S]/[I]

2. **Freeze #1 — the "parolee hold" (2025-02-14 → mid-June 2025).** A USCIS memo signed 2025-02-14 by Acting Deputy Director Andrew Davidson imposed an "immediate USCIS-wide administrative hold on all pending benefit requests filed by aliens who are or were paroled into the United States under the U4U, CHNV, or FRP processes, pending vetting for fraud, public safety, and national security concerns." [S] A March-2025 CAA filer was under it from day one. **Judge Indira Talwani (D. Mass.) lifted it on 2025-05-28** in *Svitlana Doe v. Noem*, No. 1:25-cv-10495, by class-wide preliminary relief that blocked "the suspension of immigration benefits adjudications for Parole Program recipients". [S] USCIS's 2025-06-09 "Alfonso-Royals memo" told officers to adjudicate "once USCIS has completed the additional vetting requirement"; holds were cleared in USCIS systems by 2025-06-19, with the last (fraud/national-security-flagged) cases released 2025-06-25. [S] The government appealed on 2025-07-28 and **voluntarily dismissed that appeal on 2025-11-04**, so the May 28 relief is final. [S]

3. **Between the freezes (late June → 2025-12-01)** USCIS worked the parolee backlog with "additional vetting". This is the window in which the reference case got its (c)(9) EAD (September 2025). Anything still undecided on 2025-12-02 fell into Freeze #2. [S]/[I]

4. **Freeze #2 — the "country hold" (2025-12-02 → 2026-06-12; backstopped by a class injunction from 2026-08-24).** After the 2025-11-26 shooting of two National Guard members in Washington by an Afghan national, USCIS Director Joseph Edlow announced a re-examination of green cards for nationals of the 19 "countries of concern" of Proclamation 10949 — **Cuba is one of them** — and on 2025-12-02 issued **PM-602-0192**, holding every pending benefit request filed by anyone whose country of birth or citizenship is on that list, plus all asylum applications, plus a "comprehensive re-review" of benefits already approved. **PM-602-0194 (2026-01-01)** extended it to the 39 countries of Proclamation 10998, "regardless of entry date". [S] **Chief Judge John J. McConnell Jr. (D.R.I.) vacated the policies nationwide on 2026-06-05** in *Dorcas International Institute of Rhode Island v. USCIS*, No. 1:26-cv-00132 (135-page opinion; Rule 54(b) judgment 2026-06-11). USCIS said on 2026-06-12 it "strongly disagrees" but would "follow its terms pending possible further judicial review" and that the policies "should be treated as if they are not in effect"; it appealed (1st Cir. No. 26-1703). The district court refused a stay on 2026-07-15; the **First Circuit on 2026-08-14 stayed the vacatur only as to the "Comprehensive Re-Review Policy"** and otherwise denied a stay — so the hold on *pending* cases stayed dead. [S] Independently, **Judge Charles Breyer (N.D. Cal.) on 2026-08-24** certified a nationwide class in *Red Eagle Law, L.C. v. Edlow*, No. 3:26-cv-04850, and enjoined PM-602-0192/0194, ordering USCIS to "resume normal adjudication" for class members; USCIS announced compliance on 2026-08-28. [S]

5. **Cuban-specific confirmation.** In a 2026-07-16 letter to Rep. María Elvira Salazar, USCIS said it was "taking immediate steps to lift all remaining final adjudication holds previously in place", that "processing for Cubans and Venezuelans should resume accordingly", and that it is "examining options for individuals under the Cuban Adjustment Act" — without saying what options, when, or how many people are affected. [S] (Diario Las Américas, 2026-07-22: "USCIS levanta las pausas que frenaban trámites migratorios de cubanos y venezolanos".)

6. **"Found unconstitutional"?** No. Every ruling that ended a freeze was a **statutory** ruling under the Administrative Procedure Act and the Immigration and Nationality Act — "unlawful", "arbitrary and capricious", "exceeded statutory authority". Judge Kobick (D. Mass., 2026-04-30) called the policies "discriminatory and unlawful" for ~200 plaintiffs, and Judge McConnell wrote that the freeze fell on people "solely by the happenstance of their birth", but no court is reported to have reached a constitutional holding. [S]/[I]

7. **Status on 2026-10-20 for a CAA I-485 filed March 2025 with an EAD issued September 2025:** no hold is legally in force; the case has been adjudicable since 2026-06-12 at the latest (USCIS itself was still "lifting remaining holds" in mid-July), and *Red Eagle* independently requires adjudication "in the normal course". But there is no court deadline, and output is a trickle: 139 CAA approvals and 447 denials in April–June 2026 (23.7 % approval), 89 approvals in June and 308 in July 2026, ~342,000–355,000 CAA cases pending at 2026-06-30, FY2026 national median 11.8 months (through 2026-07-31) versus 7.0 in FY2025. Attorneys report cases sitting undecided for months after interviews, more interviews, and some files routed to a central "Vetting Center". Remaining head-winds: the *Dorcas* appeal (a reversal would be the biggest downside), the re-review policy (revived by the First Circuit stay), the 2026-03-30 "strengthened screening and vetting" holds on individual "not sufficiently vetted" cases, and the 2026-05-21 "extraordinary discretion" adjustment memo (PM-602-0199), which one court reportedly found does not apply to CAA cases. [S]/[I]

### 5.2 Why May 28 matters more than April 14 for this app [I]

- April 14 protected **parole status and the (c)(11) EAD**; the Supreme Court stayed it six weeks later and the First Circuit vacated it in September. Net effect: CHNV parole ended anyway.
- May 28 protected **the right to a decision** on applications already filed (I-485, I-765, I-589, I-821, I-131…). It was never stayed, never reversed, and the appeal was dropped. **This is the order that un-froze the reference case's EAD.**

### 5.3 Legal basis (plain English)

The court treated the hold as reviewable agency action under the Administrative Procedure Act and found it likely unlawful: an indefinite, unexplained, blanket suspension of adjudications. Summaries uniformly describe it as an APA ruling, not a constitutional one. [S] for the characterization; [T] for the reasoning detail.

## 12. Was any of this "unconstitutional"?

| Ruling | Legal ground as reported | Constitutional holding? | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talwani, 2025-05-28 (*Doe v. Noem*) | APA — unexplained, indefinite blanket hold | No | [S]/[T] |
| Kobick, 2026-04-30 (*Doe v. Trump*) | "discriminatory and unlawful"; conflicts with Congress's direction that USCIS decide applications | Not reported as a constitutional holding | [S] |
| Chutkan (D.D.C.) | APA — likely arbitrary and capricious; unreasonable delay | No | [S] |
| McConnell, 2026-06-05 (*Dorcas*) | APA / INA — exceeds statutory authority; arbitrary and capricious; vacatur | No; claims pleaded under INA and APA | [S] |
| Breyer, 2026-08-24 (*Red Eagle*) | APA / INA duty to adjudicate; class-wide PI | No | [S] |

**Bottom line [I]:** the freezes were undone because courts found them **unlawful under the APA and the INA**, not because any court held them unconstitutional. The Cuban-press verbs — *anular*, *invalidar*, *tumbar* — are where the "unconstitutional" framing comes from. For the app: say "a federal court struck down (vacated) the pause" and link the order; avoid "unconstitutional". The distinction also drives risk: an APA vacatur can be reversed on appeal or mooted by a re-issued policy.

## 15. Implications for the app (design, data pipeline, estimator)

1. **Model policy regimes, not one processing time.** Hard-code these windows as time-varying covariates and show them in a "what happened to your case" strip:
   - R0 "normal": filing → 2025-02-13 (CAA median 3–5 months) [T]
   - **R1 parolee hold: 2025-02-14 → 2025-06-09** (use 2025-06-25 as the operational end for sensitivity); applies only to CHNV/U4U/FRP parolees
   - R2 catch-up with vetting: 2025-06-25 → 2025-12-01
   - **R3 country hold: 2025-12-02 → 2026-06-12** (use 2026-07-16 or 2026-08-24 as alternative ends); applies to every Cuban-born applicant regardless of entry channel
   - R4 post-vacatur restart under individualized vetting, PM-602-0199 and appeal risk: 2026-06-12 → present
   Use *adjudicable days* (calendar days minus R1 and R3 days for the applicable population) as the age regressor; the reference case has ≈ 256–306 adjudicable days, not 584.
2. **Two populations, two hold rules.** R1 keyed on *parole program* (ask: CHNV? CBP One? FRP? other?); R3 keyed on *country of birth / nationality* (all Cubans). A Cuban who entered on a visa was hit by R3 only; a Haitian CHNV parolee by both; a Cuban CHNV parolee by both.
3. **Milestone semantics.** EAD = (c)(9) card, not the revoked (c)(11); a June-2025 parole-termination notice is not a negative signal for the I-485; "interview scheduled" and "interview completed, no decision" are R4 signals worth separate fields (attorneys report long post-interview waits).
4. **Approval probability as a first-class output.** Headline CAA approval rates collapsed (52.6 % → 23.7 % quarter-on-quarter; 97 %+ historically) mainly because eligible parolee cases were frozen while clear denials and I-220A denials were issued. Show "when" and "whether" separately with the selection caveat; re-estimate when FY2026 Q4 / FY2027 Q1 tables arrive (≈ Jan and Apr 2027).
5. **Alerts feed, ranked by impact:** (a) 1st Cir. No. 26-1703 (*Dorcas*) — a reversal is the biggest downside; (b) N.D. Cal. 3:26-cv-04850 (*Red Eagle*) and any Ninth Circuit activity; (c) USCIS newsroom "court order" / "litigation-related update" alerts; (d) USCIS Policy Memoranda page (new PM-602-xxxx; the 2026-06-12 "updated instructions"); (e) Federal Register presidential documents (new proclamations change the country list); (f) D. Mass. 1:25-cv-10495 (*Doe v. Noem*) and the PM-602-0199 suit (1:26-cv-14527); (g) M.D. Fla. 8:26-cv-1314 (*Acosta Reynoso*); (h) Cubanet, CiberCuba, Directorio Cubano, Periódico Cubano, Cuba en Miami, Diario de Cuba, Diario Las Américas, Telemundo 51 as same-day leading indicators.
6. **Honest copy about the law.** The freezes were ended under the APA/INA, not the Constitution; say "a federal court struck down (vacated) the pause" and link the order.
7. **EAD-renewal logic.** Compute expiry from the issue date, warn at 180 days, and state that renewals filed on/after 2025-10-30 do not auto-extend (final rule 2026-10-14).
8. **Data vintage labels.** Every official number (median 11.8 months; 342,360 pending) must carry its as-of date and the "completed cases only" caveat.
