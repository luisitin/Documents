### 02 — CAA eligibility
Statute (Pub. L. 89-732, 8 U.S.C. 1255 note): native or citizen of Cuba; inspected and admitted or paroled (not I-220A); one year of physical presence ("un año y un día" in community usage); admissible; discretion. Derivatives (spouse/child of any nationality, CU7). 30-month rollback of the LPR date. No visa number, no priority date, public-charge exempt (confirmed later by the Aug 18 2026 Policy Manual table — see doc 11). Forms and fees as of 2026: I-485 $1,440 paper / $950 under-14 with parent; I-765 (c)(9) $260 (or $0 when filed with I-485 before Apr 1 2024 rules); I-131 $630; biometrics now bundled. Milestone sequence: receipt → biometrics → (c)(9) EAD/AP → interview notice (now expected for 2025+ filers) → RFE/NOID → decision → card. Class codes CU6 (principal) / CU7 (derivative). Notes that the CAA I-485 is filed on the generic Form I-485 with no CAA subtype in USCIS processing-times data.

## Part 2 — Executive summary

### 2.1 What CHNV was and what happened to it

CHNV was a Biden-era humanitarian parole process announced Jan 5 2023 (Venezuela had a pilot from Oct 12 2022) that let nationals of Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua and Venezuela with a US financial supporter (Form I-134A) fly to a US port of entry and be paroled for up to two years under INA 212(d)(5), with a (c)(11) EAD [S — doc 01 §1–2; `https://www.dhs.gov/news/2023/01/05/dhs-continues-prepare-end-title-42-announces-new-border-enforcement-measures`]. Through Dec 2024 CBP reported ~531,690 arrivals, including 110,240 Cubans [S — doc 05 §3; CBP monthly update]. The process was capped at 30,000/month, paused Jul–Aug 2024 over supporter fraud, and DHS announced on Oct 4 2024 that no re-parole would be offered [S — doc 01 §4].

On Jan 20 2025 EO 14165 ordered parole programs ended; CBP One appointments were cancelled Jan 23 2025; DHS published the termination notice on Mar 25 2025 (FR 2025-05128) making parole end Apr 24 2025 [S — doc 01 §5, doc 11 §3; `https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/25/2025-05128/termination-of-parole-processes-for-cubans-haitians-nicaraguans-and-venezuelans`]. Judge Talwani (D. Mass., Doe v. Noem 1:25-cv-10495-IT) stayed the categorical termination on Apr 14 2025; the Supreme Court stayed her order on May 30 2025 (No. 24A1079), letting the termination take effect; USCIS/DHS sent termination e-mails Jun 12 2025 and the First Circuit vacated the Apr 14 order on Sep 12 2025 [S — doc 03 §2, doc 11 §3; `https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a1079_7m58.pdf`]. In practice: CHNV parole and (c)(11) EADs are terminated; the only durable path for a Cuban CHNV parolee is the CAA I-485 (or asylum/TPS-less alternatives). The separate Family Reunification Parole (FRP) program was terminated Dec 15 2025 (FR 2025-22744, parole ending Jan 14 2026) with an exception for people who had an I-485 pending by Dec 15 2025 [S — doc 11 §3; `https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/15/2025-22744`].

### 2.2 What the CAA requires and how a CHNV Cuban qualifies

The Cuban Adjustment Act (Pub. L. 89-732, Nov 2 1966) lets a native or citizen of Cuba who was **inspected and admitted or paroled** into the US, has been **physically present for at least one year**, and is **admissible** adjust to LPR at the Attorney General's discretion; the spouse and child (any nationality) may adjust with the principal [S — doc 02 §1; `https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-a-cuban-native-or-citizen`]. A CHNV Cuban qualifies because CHNV entry was a parole under 212(d)(5): one year after the parole date (the I-94 "paroled" date), they file Form I-485 with the "Cuban Adjustment Act" box, a copy of the I-94, Cuban passport/birth certificate, I-693, two photos, and optionally I-765 (c)(9) and I-131 [S — doc 02 §3]. No visa number or priority date is needed, the public-charge ground does not apply (USCIS Policy Manual public-charge table, updated Aug 18 2026, keeps CAA exempt [S — doc 11 §6; doc 10 §8]), and the LPR date is rolled back up to 30 months to the later of arrival or 30 months before approval [S — doc 02 §4]. Expired parole does **not** break eligibility: the statute asks only for a past parole; USCIS's Jun 2025 termination notices expressly said pending I-485s continue [S — doc 03 §4; doc 11 §9 rumour control]. What *does* exclude a Cuban: I-220A release (not parole, Matter of Cabrera-Fernández, 27 I&N Dec. 180 (BIA 2023)), entry without inspection, inadmissibility, or a criminal/security bar [S — doc 02 §2].

Fees (I-485 $1,440; I-765 (c)(9) $260; I-131 $630; no H.R.1 surcharge on these) and the milestone sequence are in `00-key-facts-for-the-app.md`.

### 2.3 Exactly what the 2025 freezes were, what court undid them, when, and status today

**Freeze #1 — the parolee benefit hold (R1, 2025-02-14 → 2025-05-28/06-09).** An internal USCIS memo of Feb 14 2025 ("administrative hold on benefit requests filed by aliens who are or were paroled under CHNV, U4U, FRP…") stopped adjudication of I-485/I-765/I-131 filed by CHNV parolees pending vetting [S — doc 03 §3; CBS/Reuters coverage; `https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/...` not verified]. Judge Talwani ordered the hold lifted on May 28 2025 (Doe v. Noem ECF ~160) and USCIS announced it would resume processing with systems releases reported Jun 9, Jun 19 and Jun 25 2025 [S — doc 03 §3.3; doc 11 §3; `https://www.aila.org/library/...doe-v-noem...`]. The government's appeal of the May 28 order was voluntarily dismissed Nov 4 2025 [S′ — doc 11 §7 via Justice Action Center litigation tracker]. March 2025 CAA filers sat in this hold for ~3.5–4 months.

**Freeze #2 — the country-specific hold-and-review (R3, 2025-12-02 → 2026-06-05/12).** After the Nov 26 2025 National Guard shooting, USCIS issued PM-602-0192 (Dec 2 2025) placing a hold on all pending benefit requests of nationals of the 19 countries in Proclamation 10949 (Cuba included), ordering a comprehensive re-review of approved cases, and PM-602-0194 (Jan 1 2026) extended it to 39 countries under Proclamation 10998; PA-2025-26 made "country-specific factors" a negative discretionary factor [S — doc 03 §5; doc 11 §4; `https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/PM-602-0192-Hold-on-Benefit-Requests-for-Aliens-from-Countries-of-Concern.pdf`]. Cuban I-485 approvals fell to 41 (Dec 2025), 15 (Jan 2026), 10 (Feb), 25 (Mar), 23 (Apr), 27 (May) [F — doc 04/11 monthly CSV]. **Dorcas International Institute of Rhode Island v. USCIS** (D.R.I. 1:26-cv-00132, Chief Judge McConnell) vacated the Global Asylum Hold, the Benefits Hold, the Comprehensive Re-Review and the Country-Specific Factors policies under the APA on Jun 5 2026, entered Rule 54(b) final judgment Jun 11, and USCIS posted a compliance alert Jun 11/12 2026 [S — doc 03 §6; doc 11 §4; `https://www.aila.org/library/district-court-denies-government-motion-to-stay-decision-vacating-uscis-hold-policies-in-dorcas-v-uscis`; `https://www.immigrationissues.com/uscis-confirms-it-will-comply-with-the-dorcas-vacatur-of-the-country-hold-policies/`]. The government appealed (1st Cir. 26-1703, Jun 12); D.R.I. denied a stay Jul 15; the First Circuit on Aug 14 2026 granted a **partial** stay limited to the Comprehensive Re-Review Policy (already-approved cases), leaving the hold vacatur in force [S — doc 03 §6, doc 07 §15, doc 11 §7; `https://wolfsdorf.com/dorcas-on-appeal-district-court-pauses-its-own-ruling-as-first-circuit-takes-up-the-case/`]. Separately, **Red Eagle Law v. Edlow** (N.D. Cal. 3:26-cv-04850-CRB, Judge Breyer) certified a nationwide class of nationals of the 39 countries with applications not finally adjudicated as of Aug 24 2026 and enjoined PM-602-0192/0194; USCIS said it would comply; the case itself is stayed pending the Dorcas appeal (status conference Sep 25 2026) [S — doc 11 §7; today's search: `https://www.aila.org/library/district-court-issues-preliminary-injunction-to-lift-adjudication-hold-for-travel-ban-countries`, `https://mwlawllc.com/uscis-adjudication-holds-court-order-2026/`]. Today's searches found **no** reported First Circuit merits decision and **no** post-Sep-25 Red Eagle development [S — searches 2026-10-20].

**The post-freeze "discretion regime" (R4, 2026-05-21 → today).** No hold is in force, but: PM-602-0199 (May 21/22 2026) reframes adjustment as extraordinary discretionary relief when consular processing is available — with a carve-out that practitioners (CLINIC, ILRC, VisaPlace) read as excluding CAA applicants, who have no consular alternative [S — doc 07 §15, doc 11 §5; `https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/05/uscis-issues-new-policy-memorandum-on-adjustment-of-status`; `https://manifestlaw.com/news/uscis-policy-memorandum-adjustment-of-status-05-22-2026`]; PA-2026-05 (Aug 5 2026) allows denial without RFE/NOID and shortens RFE windows to ≤12 weeks [S — doc 11 §5]; the Sep 18 2026 I-485 edition implements the public-charge final rule (CAA still exempt) [S — doc 11 §6; `https://www.fredlaw.com/alert-uscis-revises-i-485-permanent-residence-adjustment-application`]. A D. Mass. suit (reported as AAUP v. Mullin, 1:26-cv-14527, ~Oct 5 2026) challenges PM-602-0199 and PA-2026-05; today's search could not find the complaint, and one result says no preliminary-relief ruling had issued by mid-October [S — `https://www.visaverge.com/greencard/cuban-green-card-approvals-drop-to-237-as-trump-administration-tightens-uscis-rules/`; doc 07 §15 — treat filing details as [T]]. Approvals have resumed only slowly: 89 (Jun 2026), 308 (Jul), 386 (Aug) against 355,520 pending, and the FY26 Q3 approval share was 23.7% (139 approved / 447 denied) [F — doc 04/11; S — `https://en.cibercuba.com/noticias/2026-10-20-u1-e135253-s27061-nid343309-solo-139-cubanos-obtuvieron-residencia-ley-ajuste`].

**Status today for a March/April 2025 CAA I-485 filer.** Legally adjudicable since Jun 2026; a Red Eagle class member; no hold applies; USCIS case-inquiry dates for I-485 "131A-FAM" at MIA (2023-06-17) / HIA (2024-07-05) mean the case is still "within normal processing" and an e-Request is rejected [S — doc 04 §5, doc 12 §4]; ~19 months pending of which ~10 were inside holds (306 adjudicable days through 2026-10-20 using R1 end Jun 9 and R3 end Jun 12; 256 using the later operational dates) [I — doc 03 §8, doc 08 T11].

### 2.4 Reference case, stage by stage

Case: Cuban CHNV parolee, I-485 (CAA) filed March 2025, (c)(9) EAD received September 2025, no I-485 decision as of October 2026; peers filed at the same time got EADs on different dates or not at all.

| Stage | Most likely explanation | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Filing (Mar 2025) | Filed into R1: the Feb 14 2025 parolee hold was already in force, so the receipt was issued (lockbox intake continued) but no biometrics/adjudication action beyond intake until the hold ended. Mar 2025 was the peak receipt month (26,187 Cuban I-485s) [F]. | High [S] |
| Hold #1 ends (May 28 → Jun 9/25 2025) | Judge Talwani's May 28 order lifted the parolee hold; USCIS releases between Jun 9 and Jun 25 moved the Mar 2025 vintage into normal queues; biometrics appointments for Mar filers clustered Jun–Aug 2025 (community reports, doc 06/07). | High [S] |
| (c)(9) EAD Sep 2025 | Issued in R2 (catch-up regime) once the I-765's identity/security vetting cleared. ### 2.6 Recommended estimation approach

Use doc 08's **VPH-1 vintage-pool hazard model**, not a FIFO "cases ahead ÷ throughput" model: (1) reconstruct the pending pool by filing vintage from the monthly series (≈175k cases older than Mar 2025 are still pending; the Mar 2025 vintage is ~88% unresolved); (2) subtract hold days per regime (R1 for CHNV, R3 for all Cubans) to get *adjudicable age*; (3) apply an age-priority hazard with β ≈ 0.037/month; (4) shrink office multipliers toward 1 with a Gamma–Poisson prior (k₀ = 50, clamp [0.5, 2]); (5) propagate a scenario grid over future monthly completions (S0 800 flat, S1 ramp to 4,600, S2 ramp to 9,200, S3 six-month re-freeze) with explicit probabilities that court/policy alerts re-weight; (6) present quantiles (q10/q25/q50/q75) and P(decision by date), never a single date; (7) backtest with CRPS/Brier/PIT on the FY2024–FY2025 vintages. Crowdsourced timelines (doc 06 schema) refine milestone-to-milestone conditionals only when n ≥ 5 per cell.

### 2.8 Top 10 things the app must get right

1. **Regime-aware age.** Show calendar age *and* adjudicable age; the breakpoints are 2025-02-14, 2025-06-09, 2025-12-02, 2026-06-12 (plus 2026-05-22, 2026-08-14, 2026-08-24, 2026-09-18 as policy flags).
2. **Headline numbers from the monthly CSV only**, with `cut_date` stored for every median (11.8 vs 12.3 depends on the cut).
3. **Entry-channel gating.** CHNV / CBP One / FRP / CFRP = paroled → CAA-eligible; I-220A = not paroled → show the Cabrera-Fernández warning, not an estimate.
4. **Probabilistic output** (quantiles and P(by date)), bilingual, with the official USCIS 80% figure and case-inquiry date shown alongside — never a single promised date.
5. **No CAA subtype in USCIS processing times** — label the 131A-FAM office figure as a proxy.
6. **EAD logic.** (c)(9) card validity (5 years if issued before 2025-12-05, 18 months after); no 540-day auto-extension for renewals filed on/after 2025-10-30; renewal reminder at 180 days before expiry; May 2027 lapse wave.
7. **Office mapping by ZIP → county → office**, codes read from the tool at runtime (KEN vs KND, Hollywood vs Oakland Park), never hard-coded addresses as truth.
8. **Litigation flags that re-weight scenarios:** Dorcas 1st Cir. 26-1703 outcome, Red Eagle PI status, AAUP v. Mullin, any new hold memo naming Cuba.
9. **Privacy by construction.** Masked receipt (`WAC12345XXXXX`), no names, n ≥ 5 cell suppression, delete-my-account RPC, no case-status polling without explicit opt-in and Torch credentials.
