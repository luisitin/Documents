## Part 2 — Executive summary

### 2.1 What CHNV was and what happened to it

CHNV was a Biden-era humanitarian parole process announced Jan 5 2023 (Venezuela pilot from Oct 12 2022) that let nationals of Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua and Venezuela with a US financial supporter (Form I-134A) fly to a US airport and be paroled for up to two years under INA 212(d)(5), with a (c)(11) EAD [S — doc 01 §1–2; `https://www.dhs.gov/news/2023/01/05/dhs-continues-prepare-end-title-42-announces-new-border-enforcement-measures`]. Through Dec 2024 CBP reported ~531,690 arrivals, **110,240 of them Cubans** [S — doc 05 §2; CBP Dec 2024 update]. The process was capped at 30,000/month, paused Jul–Aug 2024 over supporter fraud, and DHS said on Oct 4 2024 that no re-parole would be offered [S — doc 01 §4].

In parallel, about as many Cubans again — **≈100,000 (range 70k–130k, Low confidence)** — were paroled at land ports after **CBP One** appointments between Jan 12 2023 and Jan 19 2025 (I-94 class "DT") [I — doc 16 §2]. Together the two paroled cohorts (~200k Cubans) are the CAA filers of the 2024–2026 vintages.

On Jan 20 2025 EO 14165 ordered categorical parole ended and CBP One parole stopped; DHS published the CHNV termination notice Mar 25 2025 (FR 2025-05128, parole ending Apr 24 2025) [S — docs 01, 11; `https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/25/2025-05128/termination-of-parole-processes-for-cubans-haitians-nicaraguans-and-venezuelans`]. Judge Talwani (*Doe v. Noem*, D. Mass. 1:25-cv-10495-IT) stayed the categorical termination Apr 14 2025; the Supreme Court stayed her order May 30 2025 (24A1079); DHS sent termination e-mails Jun 12 2025 revoking (c)(11) EADs; the First Circuit vacated the Apr 14 order Sep 12 2025 [S — docs 01, 03, 11; `https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a1079_7m58.pdf`]. CBP One parolees were terminated by e-mail Apr 7–11 2025; *Doe v. DHS* (D. Mass. 1:25-cv-12245, Burroughs) vacated those e-mails for a certified class on Mar 31 2026, but DHS issued individualized re-termination notices from Apr 24 2026 and a later order found it "timely complying"; one single-source report says the court let the re-terminations stand [S — doc 16 §4; `https://clearinghouse.net/case/46889/`; search 2026-10-20]. The Family Reunification Parole programs were terminated Dec 15 2025 (90 FR 58032 / FR 2025-22744, parole ending Jan 14 2026) **except** for anyone with an I-485 pending by Dec 15 2025, whose parole continues until expiry or final I-485 adjudication [S — confirmed today: `https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-15/html/2025-22744.htm`]; a Jan 2026 PI partially suspended that termination [S — doc 11]. Since Aug 11 2026 (*CHIRLA v. Mullin*, D.C. Cir. 25-5289) former port-of-entry parolees are again exposed to expedited removal [S — doc 16 §6]. Net: for a Cuban CHNV or CBP One parolee, parole and the parole EAD are over; **the CAA I-485 is the path**, and the app's enforcement warnings must reflect the Aug 2026 reversal.

### 2.2 What the CAA requires and how a CHNV (or CBP One) Cuban qualifies

The Cuban Adjustment Act (Pub. L. 89-732, Nov 2 1966) lets a native or citizen of Cuba who was **inspected and admitted or paroled** into the US, has been **physically present for at least one year**, and is **admissible** adjust to LPR "in [the Attorney General's] discretion"; the spouse and child (any nationality) may adjust with the principal [S — doc 02 §1; `https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-a-cuban-native-or-citizen`]. A CHNV or CBP One Cuban qualifies because both entries were 212(d)(5) paroles: one year after the I-94 parole date they file Form I-485 (CAA box) with the I-94, Cuban passport/birth certificate, I-693, photos, and optionally I-765 (c)(9) and I-131 [S — doc 02 §3; doc 16 §5]. No visa number or priority date; the public-charge ground does not apply (Policy Manual table Aug 18 2026 keeps CAA exempt [S — docs 10/11]); the LPR date rolls back up to 30 months [S — doc 02 §4]. **The parole test is historical** — expired, terminated or revoked parole (Jun 2025 CHNV, Apr 2025/Apr 2026 CBP One) does not defeat eligibility; USCIS's own termination notices said pending I-485s continue, and CAA approvals for CBP One entrants were reported in June 2026 [S/I — docs 03, 11, 16 §5]. What excludes a Cuban: I-220A release (not parole — *Cabrera-Fernández*, 28 I&N Dec. 747; *Bello-Rubio* survived a motion to dismiss on Sep 10 2026 but no court has held I-220A = parole), conditional parole under INA 236, entry without inspection, inadmissibility [S — doc 02 §2; doc 16; search 2026-10-20].

Two 2026 instruments shape *how* the discretion is exercised (doc 14): **PM-602-0199 formally does not reach CAA cases** (it is built on §245(a) and the availability of consular processing, which a CAA applicant lacks; the government told the Tampa court so) but the general discretion framework and the "parole history" factor do; **PA-2026-05 does reach them** — since Aug 5 2026 a thin CAA packet can be denied without an RFE. Fees and the milestone sequence are in `00-key-facts-for-the-app.md` §2–4.

### 2.3 Exactly what the 2025 freezes were, what court undid them, when, and status today

**Freeze #1 — the parolee benefit hold (R1, 2025-02-14 → 2025-05-28/06-09).** The Davidson memo of Feb 14 2025 (*Administrative Hold on All USCIS Benefit Requests Filed by Parolees Under U4U, CHNV or FRP*) stopped adjudication of every benefit request filed by those parolees pending vetting [S — doc 03 §3; doc 16 §3; official copy `https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/foia/AdministrativeHoldonAllUSCISBenefitRequestsFiledbyParoleesUnderU4U_CHNVorFRP.pdf`]. **It named only U4U, CHNV and FRP — CBP One, visa and I-220A entrants were not covered on paper** (doc 16; low-confidence flag because the vetting rationale could have been applied operationally). Judge Talwani stayed the hold on May 28 2025 (dkt 107); USCIS's Jun 9 2025 memo authorised adjudication once vetting was complete, with system releases reported Jun 19 and Jun 25 [S — docs 03, 11; `https://justiceactioncenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/107-Second-Stay-Order.pdf`]. The government's appeal of the May 28 order was dismissed Nov 4 2025 [S′ — doc 11]. March 2025 CHNV filers sat in this hold ~3.5–4 months.

**Freeze #2 — the country hold-and-review (R3, 2025-12-02 → 2026-06-05/12).** PM-602-0192 (Dec 2 2025) held all pending benefit requests of nationals of the 19 Proclamation-10949 countries incl. Cuba and ordered re-review of approvals; PM-602-0194 (Jan 1 2026) extended it to 39 countries; PA-2025-26 added "country-specific factors" [S — docs 03, 11; `https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/PM-602-0192-Hold-on-Benefit-Requests-for-Aliens-from-Countries-of-Concern.pdf`]. **This one keyed on nationality, so every Cuban I-485 was held regardless of entry channel.** Cuban approvals fell to 41 / 15 / 10 / 25 / 23 / 27 per month Dec 2025–May 2026 [F — doc 04]. *Dorcas Int'l Inst. of R.I. v. USCIS* (D.R.I. 1:26-cv-00132, McConnell) vacated the Benefits Hold, Global Asylum Hold, Comprehensive Re-Review and Country-Specific Factors policies under the APA on **Jun 5 2026**, entered Rule 54(b) judgment Jun 11, and USCIS posted its compliance alert Jun 11/12 [S — docs 03, 11; `https://blog.cyrusmehta.com/2026/06/dorcas-v-uscis-federal-court-reaffirms-that-uscis-must-adjudicate-not-stonewall-immigration-benefits.html`; `https://www.immigrationissues.com/uscis-confirms-it-will-comply-with-the-dorcas-vacatur-of-the-country-hold-policies/`]. The government appealed (1st Cir. 26-1703, Jun 12); a stay was denied Jul 15 (D.R.I. per AILA; attribution still disputed by one site); on Aug 14 the First Circuit granted a **partial** stay limited to the Comprehensive Re-Review Policy [S — docs 03, 07, 11; `https://wolfsdorf.com/dorcas-on-appeal-district-court-pauses-its-own-ruling-as-first-circuit-takes-up-the-case/`]. Independently, *Red Eagle Law v. Edlow* (N.D. Cal. 3:26-cv-04850-CRB, Breyer) certified on **Aug 24 2026** a nationwide class of nationals of the 39 countries with applications not finally adjudicated that day and enjoined PM-602-0192/0194; USCIS said Aug 28 it "strongly disagrees" but will comply; the case is stayed pending the *Dorcas* appeal [S — docs 11, 15; `https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/recent-court-order-on-hold-policies`; `https://clearinghouse.net/case/48417/`]. **Today's searches found no First Circuit briefing, argument or merits ruling in 26-1703, no post-Sep-25 *Red Eagle* order and no Ninth Circuit appeal** [S — searches 2026-10-20].

**The "discretion regime" (R4, policy 2026-05-21/22, operational 2026-06-12 → today).** No hold is in force, but: PM-602-0199 (May 21/22 2026) reframes §245(a) adjustment as extraordinary relief — formally out of scope for CAA cases (doc 14) [S — `https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/PM-602-0199-AdjustmentOfStatusAndDiscretion-20260521.pdf`]; **PA-2026-05 (Aug 5 2026)** allows denial without RFE/NOID for every pending request incl. ### 2.6 Recommended estimation approach

Use doc 08's **VPH-1 vintage-pool hazard model**, not a FIFO "cases ahead ÷ throughput" model: (1) reconstruct the pending pool by filing vintage from the monthly series (≈175k cases older than Mar 2025 still pending; the Mar 2025 vintage ~88% unresolved); (2) subtract hold days per regime — **R1 only for CHNV/U4U/FRP entrants, R3 for every Cuban** (doc 16) — to get adjudicable age; (3) apply an age-priority hazard with β ≈ 0.037/month; (4) shrink office multipliers toward 1 (Gamma–Poisson, k₀ = 50, clamp [0.5, 2]); (5) propagate a scenario grid over future completions (S0 800 flat, S1 ramp to 4,600, S2 ramp to 9,200, S3 six-month re-freeze) with probabilities that court/policy alerts re-weight; (6) present quantiles and P(decision by date), never a single date, next to the official 80% figure and case-inquiry date; (7) backtest with CRPS/Brier/PIT on FY2024–FY2025 vintages. Round-2 amendments: mandamus multiplier ×1.3 (not ×3) until crowdsourced `mandamus_outcome` data exists (doc 15); approval-given-decision prior 70–90% conditioned on packet completeness, shown with the Jul–Aug 2026 population shares (49%/47%) (doc 14); cohort prior adds ≈100k CBP One Cubans and tests channel as the first covariate for EAD timing (doc 16); tests T21/T22 added.
