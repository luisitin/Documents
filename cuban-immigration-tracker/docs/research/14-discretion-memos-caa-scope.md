## 2. Why this question is hard (plain-language background)

- **Two different adjustment statutes.** Most green-card applications inside the U.S. are "adjustment of status" under **INA § 245(a)** (8 U.S.C. § 1255(a)). A Cuban under the CAA adjusts under **section 1 of Pub. L. 89-732 (1966)**, codified as a note to 8 U.S.C. § 1255 — a separate statute with its own requirements (Cuban native/citizen, inspected and admitted or paroled, one year physically present, admissible) [T; see doc 02 §1]. Both are filed on the same Form I-485, which is why USCIS data cannot separate them (doc 04).
- **Both statutes are discretionary.** INA § 245(a) says status "may be adjusted by the Attorney General, in his discretion and under such regulations as he may prescribe". The CAA uses the **identical formula** ("may be adjusted by the Attorney General, in his discretion and under such regulations as he may prescribe") [T]. So "CAA is non-discretionary" — a phrase several law-firm blogs use — is wrong as a matter of statutory text; what practitioners mean is that CAA has no visa-number, no priority date, no § 245(c) bars and a public-charge exemption, so a clean case was historically approved almost automatically (doc 02 §4, doc 07 rows for FY2023–FY2024: 97–99 % approval shares).
- **"Statutorily non-discretionary" adjustment really exists** — in statutes that say "shall adjust": NACARA § 202 (Nicaraguans/Cubans, 1997), HRIFA § 902 (Haitians, 1998), the Liberian Refugee Immigration Fairness provision (§ 7611 of the FY2020 NDAA), and refugee adjustment under INA § 209(a) [T]. These are the categories PM-602-0199 carves out in footnote 24 (§3.2 below). Note the irony: NACARA § 202 *is* "Cuban" (it covered certain Cubans present since 1995) but it is **not** the CAA.
- **The memo's premise does not fit the CAA.** PM-602-0199's full title ends "…an Extraordinary Relief that Permits Applicants to Dispense with the Ordinary Consular Visa Process" [S — JD Supra]. The "ordinary" route it points to — go home, get an immigrant visa at a consulate — does not exist for a CAA beneficiary: no consulate issues a "CAA immigrant visa"; the Act only confers adjustment on someone already in the U.S. after a year of presence [I from the statute; also the reported reasoning of the *Acosta Reynoso* court].
- **Vocabulary.** *RFE* = Request for Evidence; *NOID* = Notice of Intent to Deny; *PI* = preliminary injunction (a court order pausing a policy while the case proceeds); *TRO* = temporary restraining order (a shorter emergency version); *vacatur* = a court wiping a policy out under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA); *Policy Manual* = USCIS's binding internal guidance, organised by Volume/Part/Chapter; *Policy Alert (PA)* = the notice USCIS posts when it changes the Policy Manual; *Policy Memorandum (PM)* = a stand-alone directive to officers.

**Resolution.** The memo's footnote groups statutes whose text says "**shall** adjust". The CAA says "**may** … in his discretion" — the same words as § 245(a) [T]. A careful drafter would therefore not put the CAA in that footnote, and the two pin-cited close readings say it is not there. The blogs claiming otherwise garble the list. **Working conclusion: PM-602-0199 does not mention the Cuban Adjustment Act, Pub. L. 89-732, or non-§ 245 adjustment generally, by name — neither to include nor to exclude it** [I from [S] sources, ~80 % confidence]. Verbatim confirmation requires an agent that can open the PDF (open question O1, §11).

### 3.3 Why "not named" still means "largely out of scope" for the CAA

1. The memo's operative subject is the discretionary clause of **§ 245(a)** and the comparison with **consular processing** [S — Harris Beach, JD Supra]. A CAA applicant is not adjusting under § 245(a) and has no consular alternative [I; reported reasoning of the *Acosta Reynoso* court, §6].
2. **The government's own litigating position** (reported): in *Acosta Reynoso* the United States told the court the memorandum does not apply to CAA applications [S-secondary — CiberCuba 2026-10-08, CubaHeadlines 342267, Directorio Cubano]. An agency position stated in federal court is the closest thing to "USCIS stated a position on CAA" that exists; USCIS has issued no public CAA-specific guidance.
3. **DHS's public characterisation** that the memo is only a reminder of existing discretion [S-secondary].
4. Field practice: Cuban outlets reported new "why did you not consular-process?"-type interview questions in late May 2026 and that some field offices had stopped asking them by ~2026-06-10 (doc 11 §5, Factchequeado; single-source) [S′ carried].

### 3.4 Why "out of scope" does **not** mean "safe"

- CAA adjustment is discretionary by statute; officers always had to find that discretion favours the applicant [T].
- The general discretion framework in Vol. 7 Part A Ch. 10 (rewrite 2025-11-27) and the 2025-08-19 negative factors (anti-American ideology, antisemitism, **parole history**, social-media content) apply to every I-485 including CAA (doc 11 §5, items 7) [S carried].
- PA-2026-05 (§4) applies regardless.
- The data: FY2026 Q3 (Apr–Jun 2026) CAA completions were 139 approvals vs 447 denials (23.7 % approval) [F — doc 04 VERIFIED-FILE; doc 11; confirmed today by Cuba en Miami (447 denials), UnoTV (139 of 586), Newsweek and CiberCuba nid343309] [S]. **State it against its baseline:** FY2026 Q2 (Jan–Mar 2026) was 50 approvals vs 45 denials (52.6 %), FY2026 Q1 (Oct–Dec 2025) 4,098 vs 373 (91.7 %), and FY-to-date through Q3 is 4,287 approved / 865 denied (83 %) [F — doc 04 quarterly workbook; Q2 figures re-confirmed 2026-10-20 by Newsweek and CiberCuba nid343309 — https://www.newsweek.com/green-card-approval-rate-drops-for-3-major-categories-12578953 ; https://en.cibercuba.com/noticias/2026-10-20-u1-e135253-s27061-nid343309-solo-139-cubanos-obtuvieron-residencia-ley-ajuste]. The Q3 share is therefore driven mainly by **collapsed approval volume, not by a record number of denials**: 447 denials in a quarter is *fewer* than the same quarter of FY2025 (≈ 1,900 implied by 27,602 completions at 93.2 %) or FY2024 (≈ 660 implied by 50,627 at 98.7 %) [I — derived from the outlet-reported figures in doc 07 §3, not from the files; CiberCuba 2026-10-20 makes the same point per the fact-checker]. Pending inventory at 30 Jun 2026: 342,360 (quarterly workbook) / 355,371 (monthly CSV); the "more than 342,000 unresolved" figure in today's press (CiberCuba, CubaHeadlines 343309) is the workbook number [F — doc 04 §1.3; S]. Cuba en Miami cautions that a denial can occur for many reasons and the data do not prove a general policy of rejection [S — https://www.cubaenmiami.com/uscis-nego-mas-casos-de-ajuste-cubano-en-tres-meses-que-durante-los-seis-meses-anteriores/]. CubaHeadlines notes that the memo's non-application does not mean every Cuban application must be approved [S].

### 6.2 What the order says about PM-602-0199 and the CAA

- **Visible in the primary excerpt:** the order describes PM-602-0199 as "a memorandum reminding officers that adjustment is discretionary" (search synthesis of the ECF document returned in the *AAUP* query) [S].
- **Reported by three Cuban outlets, not visible in the excerpt:** "the court noted the Government's position that the memorandum does not apply to applications under the Cuban Adjustment Act and found that argument persuasive" (CubaHeadlines 342267, English; CiberCuba 2026-10-08, Spanish/English); Directorio Cubano: on 2026-07-30 Judge Mizelle "consideró que el memorando de mayo no se aplicaba a las solicitudes bajo la Ley de Ajuste Cubano, porque esa legislación contempla específicamente el ajuste de personas que ya están físicamente en Estados Unidos" [S-secondary — https://www.cubaheadlines.com/articles/342267 ; https://en.cibercuba.com/noticias/2026-10-08-u1-e13-s27061-nid342267-demandan-uscis-nuevas-restricciones-obtener-green-card ; https://directoriocubano.info/cuba/cada-vez-menos-residencias-ley-ajuste-cubano-fin-sin-derogarla]. The three accounts are consistent with each other and with the visible "reminding officers" language; they may share a wire source (doc 03 mentions EFE), so count them as one strong secondary report, not three independent ones [I].
- **USCIS's position:** per those reports, the **Government argued in its opposition that PM-602-0199 does not apply to CAA applications**. That is the only known statement of a USCIS/DOJ position on the memo's CAA scope. The brief itself (ECF number unknown) was not seen [S-secondary]. Open question O7.

### 6.3 Legal weight (for a non-lawyer)

- The PI was denied on **irreparable harm**, so whatever the court said about the memo was either background or part of a likelihood-of-success discussion that was not needed for the result — i.e. **dicta**, persuasive but not binding, and not an injunction against anyone [I].
- A party's litigating position is not a regulation; USCIS could in principle argue differently elsewhere, though doing so invites judicial-estoppel problems [I].
- The 2026-08-13 reporting order shows the court expected some plaintiffs' cases to be adjudicated in the interim — consistent with USCIS having resumed CAA decisions after *Dorcas* (89 approvals in June, 308 in July 2026 [F — doc 04]) [I].
- Delay suits by individual Cubans remain possible but this order is a template for **denial** of emergency relief at ~15–20 months of pendency; the app's "what can I do" screen should not imply mandamus is a quick fix (doc 11 §10 item 6 is now answered: no adjudication deadline was ordered) [I].

## 7. Synthesis — is a CHNV Cuban's CAA I-485 inside or outside the "discretion regime"?

| Instrument | Formally applies to a CAA I-485? | Evidence | Practical exposure of a clean CHNV/CAA case |

| **PA-2026-05** (2026-08-05) | **Yes** — all benefit requests pending on/after 08-05 | [S] §4 | **High** for thin packets (missing I-94/parole proof, weak nationality proof, no I-693); low for complete packets |

**Answer to the critic's question:** CAA filers are **outside PM-602-0199 but inside PA-2026-05 and inside general discretion**. The corpus's label "R4 = PM-602-0199 discretion regime" should be re-described as "R4 = general-discretion + evidentiary-denial regime", with PM-602-0199 kept only as a dated flag.

## 8. Implications for the app (concrete)

1. **Approval-given-decision prior.** The 23.7 % FY2026 Q3 headline predates PA-2026-05 and, per the government's own position, is not a PM-602-0199 effect in CAA cases; it is dominated by I-220A denials, hold-era decisions and abandoned cases (doc 07 §8, doc 08 §10). **State it against its baselines, never alone:** FY2026 Q2 52.6 % (50/95), FY2026 Q1 91.7 % (4,098/4,471), FY-to-date through Q3 83 % (4,287/5,152) [F doc 04], and a long-run anchor of ~95 % (Inmigreat's USCIS-data estimate of 95.2 % for Cuban adjustment cases FY2017–FY2026 [S — https://www.inmigreat.com/en/analytics/i-485/, surfaced today; not independently checked], consistent with the 97–99 % FY2023–24 figures in doc 07). The Q3 share is a collapsed-numerator effect — 447 denials is fewer than the same quarter of FY2024 or FY2025 (§3.4) — so the app's "share of decisions approved" tile must carry that caveat or it will read as "three in four Cubans are now refused". The 85–90 % prior for a *clean CHNV-parole CAA case* is therefore not contradicted by anything found today — but it is also unconfirmed, and PA-2026-05 adds a new, packet-quality-dependent denial channel from 2026-08-05 that **packet completeness reduces but does not eliminate** (denial is also allowed where initial evidence was technically submitted but the officer finds the burden of proof unmet, §4.1). Recommendation: keep the prior but (i) widen the interval to ~70–90 % until the FY2026 Q4 workbook (~Dec 2026) and the Sep–Nov 2026 monthly CSVs show post-08-05 behaviour, (ii) condition it on a **packet-completeness** input (I-94/parole record on file? Cuban passport or birth certificate? I-693 submitted?) while keeping a residual PA-2026-05 discount even for complete packets, and (iii) show the July–August 2026 approval share (308/626 = 49 %; 386/820 = 47 % [F doc 04]) as the population figure next to it [I].
2. **RFE/NOID screen.** Applies to every CAA I-485, I-765 and I-131 pending on or filed after 2026-08-05: RFE deadline ≤ 12 weeks (no +14 days for abroad mail), NOID ≤ 30 days (+3 mail), no extensions; and "no RFE" is no longer a good sign. Add copy: "Since 5 Aug 2026 USCIS may deny without first asking for missing documents; check that your file has X, Y, Z." Spanish: "Desde el 5 de agosto de 2026 USCIS puede negar sin pedir antes los documentos que falten; verifique que su expediente incluya…" [S].
3. **Interview-prep copy.** Drop any suggestion that the officer will ask a CAA applicant why they did not consular-process (that is the § 245(a) question; the government says the memo does not reach CAA). Keep the discretionary-factor checklist (lawful parole entry, continuous presence, no unauthorised-work issue — parolees had (c)(11) EADs —, criminal history, social-media/"anti-American" screening, parole history as a stated factor) [I from §3.4].
4. **AAUP alert rule.** Poll CourtListener docket **74919678** (`GET /api/rest/v4/docket-entries/?docket=74919678` — endpoint pattern [T], not verified today; most query patterns need an API token) and the Clearinghouse page; do **not** use the AAUP.org litigation page as a docket feed (§5.2, C11). Re-weight scenarios only on relief that touches the **Denial Policy**; log relief against the AOS Policy as informational for CAA users. Also watch for an amended complaint adding Cuban plaintiffs (would raise relevance) [I].
5. **Acosta Reynoso alert rule.** Low priority; the PI is denied and the case is a delay suit. Keep the ECF 41 link as the citation for "courts have not ordered USCIS to speed up CAA cases" and for the government's CAA-outside-the-memo position. If the mandamus multiplier in doc 08 is kept, cap it and note that this court denied emergency relief at ~15–20 months [I].
6. **Regime labels.** Rename R4 in `regimes.json` as above; keep breakpoints 2026-05-22 (memo public) and 2026-08-05 (PA-2026-05) as flags, with `applies_to_caa: false` and `true` respectively, and add `source` fields pointing to §3/§4 here [I].
7. **Rumour control strings.** "El memo del 21 de mayo elimina la Ley de Ajuste" → false; the memo does not name the CAA and the government told a federal court it does not apply to CAA cases; the Act is unchanged. "USCIS siempre manda un RFE antes de negar" → false since 2026-08-05 [S].
